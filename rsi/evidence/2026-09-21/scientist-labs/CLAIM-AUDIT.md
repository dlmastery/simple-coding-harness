# Scientific outputs and the scientist are different objects

The first study changed a feature group. The second changed the estimator recipe selected by a cheap screen. Both used the same fuller training and selection split when comparing a contribution with its ablation. The review-driven follow-up changed only the seed of that final comparison. All eight fits and all eight saved-prediction checks completed.

The weather-input repeat exceeded its predeclared 5% relative-MAE threshold. The forest replacement improved selection MAE by about 1.15% at seed 17 and 1.23% at seed 29, with greater fit cost. These are development observations on a known public task. They do not show that weather causes the observed demand changes or that the recipe is useful for advance forecasting.

The screen's January-only score cannot be compared directly with the fuller January–June score as if the evaluated data were identical. Only forest was confirmed under fuller conditions; no fuller tree ranking was established in this study. Removing the forest replacement tests the whole supplied estimator package, not the isolated effects of bagging, depth, or nonlinear representation.

The research instructions have one unchanged hash across the lineage. The author followed them while its context grew. No instruction revision, inherited revised improver, or matched old/new-researcher test occurred. The two retained model improvements are evidence about task outputs, not effective recursive self-improvement.
