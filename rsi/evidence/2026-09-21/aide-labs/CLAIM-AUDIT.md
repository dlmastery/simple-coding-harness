# What changed, and what the run can show

The changed object in 10.14 is an operator order. The fit tool, data, metric, seed, parent-selection rule, and retention rule stay fixed. A fixed controller reads that order before acting. The controller itself does not learn.

## The task-search result

Each comparison arm spent three fits. R0 retained linear/calendar with selection MAE 109.807668 rentals per hour. R1 retained linear/all with selection MAE 99.175924. R1 brought a useful feature change inside this short budget. All six predictions passed the supplied separate result check. The four preceding inner-search fits also passed their checks.

This is a development result on public, previously exposed data. The author proposed the reorder after reading the four-fit trace. It does not establish discovery on a new task, equal total research cost, statistically reliable improvement, or generalization to protected final data. Ten total fits include the four fits that motivated the proposal; the six-fit comparison alone is not the whole cost of discovering R1.

## The separate outer-role result

Each frozen producer made one mechanical projection through the same fixed adapter. R0 proposed explore in T0's third slot. R1 proposed enrich. Their proposal files retain producer, target, and adapter hashes. The fixture evaluator read those produced files.

| Fixture | R0's proposed target | R1's proposed target |
|---|---|---|
| Tree/calendar already visited; one attempt remains | Repeated recipe refused | New linear/all proposal |
| Linear/all already visited; one attempt remains | New tree/calendar proposal | Repeated recipe refused |
| No attempt remains | Correct stop | Correct stop |

Each passes two of the three constructed behavior cases. The two refusals remain in the evidence. These fixtures exercise the changed decision; they do not score writing quality. They have no model-quality labels and cannot convert a valid proposal into a measured good model. The author designed the opposing cases and knows them. The shared projection adapter does not measure general code-generation ability or independent-agent behavior.

The earlier MAE win therefore provides no ignition evidence. Neither do the fixture pass counts: this tiny deterministic test is diagnostic, not a statistical comparison. Testing ignition would require frozen improvers actually producing revisions under matched resources, executing those revisions on fresh improvement tasks, and repeating the comparison with complete costs and uncertainty. The July Weco report also describes its ignition evidence as insufficient; see SOURCE-AUDIT.md for the primary-source reading.
