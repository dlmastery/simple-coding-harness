# Adapt the method to a new subject

Preserve the teaching and execution standards. Change the concept sequence and task to fit the subject. Do not turn the RSI example into a universal technical ladder.

## Choose the right task

An appropriate task is small enough to inspect, authentic enough to matter, and rich enough to expose the mechanism. It has a visible baseline, a measurable intervention, failures worth explaining, and a path to harder cases.

For ML subjects, include data and experiment design. A convenient text task is not a substitute for model training when the point is to understand learning. Conversely, a training run is unnecessary when the concept concerns only orchestration or evaluation.

Show the same scientific question at different scales. Keep the laptop exercise meaningful on its own. A large-compute extension should state which changes affect data, model, optimizer, objective, or evaluation.

## Example adaptations

These are starting design choices, not claims about the latest literature. Search the current month before selecting research or software.

| Topic | Small task to consider | Conceptual path to develop | Larger extension |
|---|---|---|---|
| RSI | Regression and classification hill climbing on small tabular data | Fixed experiment, bounded search, structured workflows, domain relations, generated harness, self-* distinctions, inherited improver, fair evaluation | Larger data and model families, distributed trials, authentic model–harness training |
| Diffusion models | Low-dimensional distributions, then a small image dataset if resources permit | Data, noise, denoising, objectives, sampling, conditioning, quality/cost tradeoffs, advanced methods | Larger networks, datasets, distributed training, and stronger evaluations |
| Generative flow methods | Transport between simple two-dimensional distributions | Density and samples, transformations or paths, objectives, learned dynamics, integration, conditioning, comparison | Larger generative models and accelerated training or sampling |
| OPSD or related self-distillation | A small verified student/teacher experiment or transparent update illustration | Confirm the intended acronym and source; then supervision, policy distributions, objective, update, failure modes, and evaluation | Authentic open-model training with declared GPU needs |

“Flow methods” can name different families. “OPSD” can be ambiguous without a source. Resolve a consequential ambiguity early while continuing task-independent planning. Do not invent an expansion and build the wrong course.

## Keep concepts distinct

Define neighboring methods through their objective, learned object, information access, feedback, and execution. Show one concrete example and a counterexample. An attractive analogy must not merge mechanisms that differ scientifically.

Teach a complex formula after the student understands the quantities it connects. Use a small numerical or visual example, then the general form, then an executable test. The agent writes implementation; the learner predicts and interprets.

## Portable course design

Avoid dependence on one coding agent, cloud provider, or GPU scheduler unless the user chose it. Use capability checks and clear adapters. Students should be able to ask for a change in resources without rewriting the scientific specification by hand.

The exact number of themes and lessons depends on prerequisites and evidence. Do not copy 99 labs or the RSI directory names into an unrelated course. Keep the walkthrough, context, illustrations, quizzes, takeaways, next steps, research freshness, and provenance standards intact.
