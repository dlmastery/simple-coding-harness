# Read assumptions before claiming acceleration

Read [Economics of Recursive Self-Improvement, v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15802v1), 14 September 2026: selected passages in sections 2.1–2.3 and the opening of section 3, PDF pages 6–9 and 15–16, on 21 September. The HTML contains mainly the introduction, so it was insufficient for this assumption check.

The models distinguish existing algorithmic efficiency from its rate of improvement. Feedback strength depends on responsiveness along the relevant production relationships. Human work, experimentation, inference, training compute, and data can constrain the loop. The empirical calibration introduces an R&D-effort aggregator, assuming its inputs interact with prior algorithmic progress in the same way. That modeling choice is not established by a benchmark improvement. Our sequential time calculator does not implement or calibrate these production models.

Also read [Recursive Criticality, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00137v1), dated 31 August, sections 2.1–2.3 and 3.1–3.3. It distinguishes local perturbation amplification from baseline throughput, with explicit delay and frontier-hardening assumptions. Its reference thresholds and scenarios illustrate dynamics; they are not empirical forecasts. Our calculation estimates none of its recursive-gain parameters.
