## Matched later evidence

Both arms start with identical S2 bytes, receive one task proposal, execute two internal checks, and then face the same three external checks. Both internally retain their child. On the fixed external cases, the v0-produced child passes microseconds and seconds but fails to refuse ticks: two of three. The v1-produced child passes all three. The declared rule therefore retains v1 for this local example. All failures remain recorded.

This supports an inherited procedural change and a narrow benefit on the constructed cases. ## One counterexample and the next test

The revised updater trades away its internal known-case regression check. A child could handle its new unit and unknown-unit refusal while breaking a known conversion. The identical external seconds case helps detect that specific regression here; it does not cover every unit or task. A useful next experiment would use a separately controlled set of real run records with missing, mixed, and ambiguous metadata, plus independent actors and repeated matched budgets. No such extension ran in this allocation.

The schedule simulation counts hypothetical costs under three update horizons. It does not measure whether slower updates improve quality or reduce noise.
